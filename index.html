Every claim in it is a claim about this game's build, and node tools/store/sdk-check.js fails if the build carries a library this page does not name, or if the game sends a gameplay stat this page does not list by name.

The short version

Boxtide is a single-player puzzle game. Your progress is a file on your own phone. The game has no account of its own and never asks for your name, email, contacts, photos or location. The game itself makes no network calls: whatever leaves the phone leaves through one of these Google services.

  • AdMob shows rewarded ads you choose to watch, and interstitial ads between levels. It starts at every launch, after Google's consent check, so that an ad is ready when you want one.
  • Firebase receives gameplay stats and crash reports so the game can be balanced and fixed, and the game asks it for a short list of tuning numbers (Remote Config). It only does so once the consent check allows it, and never while Share gameplay stats in Settings is off. It is never used for ads.
  • Play Games holds your cloud save, achievements and Night Shift score, only while you are signed in to Play Games.
  • Play Billing handles a purchase, inside Google Play's own sheet.

Nothing else in the build sends data anywhere:

  • Google Play services and Unity Gaming Services Core are the shared parts the SDKs above stand on. Unity Gaming Services Core comes in with Unity IAP; the game never starts Unity's online services, so nothing reaches Unity through it.
  • AndroidX, Kotlin and the Unity engine packages are building blocks with no network of their own.
  • Unity Mobile Notifications schedules the game's local reminders on the phone itself, and only after you say yes to them (at most one a day, never at night, off any time in Settings). Nothing leaves the phone: there is no push service and no server.
  • Unity's own analytics, crash reporting and performance reporting are switched off, and the engine is told not to send Unity hardware statistics.
  • The project's development tools (the editor bridge MCP for Unity and HavivStudios' unity-tools) collect nothing in the game.

There is no attribution SDK, no social SDK and no other ad network.

What is stored on your phone

All of it is in the app's own private folder, which no other app can read and which Android deletes when Boxtide is uninstalled.

FileWhat it is
save.jsonYour game: levels and stars, coins, cranes and boosters, dailies and streak, Night Shift bests, postcards, your home harbour, achievements, purchases (the transaction ids, so a purchase is granted only once), and your settings.
save.json.bakThe previous save.json, kept by every write so that one bad write cannot cost you the game.

Firebase and the Google SDKs keep their own small files in the same folder: Firebase's random installation ids and any stats or crash report waiting for a connection, and UMP's record of your consent answer. They go when the app is uninstalled.

The game reads no contacts, photos, files of yours, calendar, microphone or camera, and never asks for your location. It asks you for no permission at all at first launch.

Adverts (AdMob)

  • Rewarded ads are always your choice: double the coins on a result, a crane when you are one move short, a rescue in Night Shift, and the first continue after a jam. The reward is given only when the ad finishes; nothing is taken if you skip one.
  • Interstitial ads show only between levels, when you leave a finished level's result. Never in your first session, never before level 15, never when a level starts, at least 90 seconds apart and 30 seconds after a rewarded ad, and at most 6 a session and 12 a day. Remove ads (a purchase) stops them for good and keeps the rewarded ones on offer.
  • No banners.
  • Consent comes first. Before AdMob starts, the game runs Google's User Messaging Platform consent check and shows its consent form where the law requires one. Your answer decides whether ads may be personalised. If the check says ads may not be requested at all, AdMob is not started.
  • The advertising ID. AdMob reads your device's Google advertising ID (the reason for the AD_ID permission). It is Google's identifier, and you can reset or delete it.
  • Changing your mind. Settings > Ad privacy > Choices reopens the consent form where Google offers one (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland). Everywhere, Android's Settings > Privacy > Ads lets you delete your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads.
  • Pre-release builds show Google's test ads, not real ones.

Google's ad-data practices: https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

Gameplay stats and crash reports (Firebase)

The game sends its own gameplay stats to Firebase Analytics and crash reports to Firebase Crashlytics, so that one developer can see where players get stuck or stop, and what crashes, and then fix the game. They are not used for ads.

  • What a stat is. A short named event from the game: a level started or finished (its number, stars and moves), a jam, a booster or continue used, the store opened, a purchase granted (the product), an ad shown or rewarded, a postcard piece, a daily finished, a Night Shift run's score, a weekend event opened or a place finished, gold containers shipped, the language picked in Settings (and the language the game is shown in), a season pass tier reached or premium bought, the coin shop opened (which button, and the coins held), a paint, yard theme, container skin, harbour decoration or small item bought with coins (which one, its kind and its price) or put on, a streak freeze bought, a new kind of level (reefers, heavies, the tide, a priority ship) met for the first time, a home harbour task built or restyled (the task, the style, the build points or coins spent) or a harbour area finished, a session started. Each one is listed by name in the stats, by name, and described field by field in Docs/telemetry/EVENTS.md in the game's source. A stat carries only numbers, words from a fixed list and the game's own ids (a level, a product, a harbour task). Nothing you typed, no save file, no name.
  • Who it is tied to. A random id Firebase creates when the game is installed. It is not your name, not your Google account and not your advertising ID, and the game never joins it with anything else. A reinstall gets a new one.
  • Nothing before consent. The build tells Firebase, before any of the game's code runs, to store nothing and collect nothing. The game changes that only once Google's consent check has finished. Where the GDPR applies (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), stats are kept only if you agreed there to the storing of information on your device. Elsewhere they are kept unless you switch them off. Ad storage, ad user data and ad personalisation are never granted to Firebase. Crash reports follow the same answer as the stats.
  • Crash reports. A crash report covers a crash in the game's C# code or in its native (engine) code. To make a native crash readable, the developer uploads the game build's own symbol files to Crashlytics when the build is made; that upload is about the game, not about you, and sends nothing from your phone.
  • How to turn it off. Switch off Share gameplay stats in Settings. From that moment no stats and no crash reports are sent, and the answer is kept in your save until you change it. Turning down consent later in Ad privacy does the same.
  • How long it is kept. Firebase Analytics keeps event data for Google Analytics' default retention (two months); Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days. The data is not exported anywhere else.
  • Deleting it. Because the id is random and the game never shows it, there is no way to find "your" stats by your name or email. Switching off Share gameplay stats stops new ones; what was already sent expires as described above.

Tuning numbers (Firebase Remote Config)

At launch the game asks Firebase Remote Config for a short, fixed list of tuning numbers and switches: how often adverts between levels may show (only ever less often than the rules above), the Strongbox, deal and event numbers, the pace of the computer crews in a Harbour Race, and switches that can hide a coming event. The list is in the game's design files (Docs/design/REMOTE-CONFIG.md); the game ignores any other value.

  • What it sends. Firebase's random installation id, the app's id and version, and the device's language and country, so Google can pick the values. Nothing about your play, your purchases or you. The installation id is the same random id as above; it is never the advertising ID.
  • A/B tests. The developer may try two values of a number on two groups of players (Firebase A/B Testing), for example a different Strongbox size. Firebase puts your installation id in a group at random; the group is not a profile of you, and no value can change a price, take back anything you have, or sell anything random.
  • When it is off. Before the consent check allows Firebase, while Share gameplay stats is off, with no connection, or when the request takes longer than 3 seconds, the game plays by its built-in numbers. New values only take effect at the next launch.

Play Games (cloud save, achievements, Night Shift)

  • Nothing is sent unless you are signed in to Play Games. Sign-in is Google's: if your Play Games profile allows automatic sign-in, Google signs you in when the game starts; otherwise only Settings > Play Games > Sign in asks. Not signed in, the game carries on fully offline.
  • Cloud save. A copy of save.json goes into your own Play Games saved-games slot, named boxtide, so a new phone can pick up where the old one left off. If the two differ, the game merges them and keeps the better of each.
  • Achievements, the Night Shift leaderboard and Game Stats. Achievements you earn and your Night Shift best score go to Play Games. The leaderboard shows your Play Games name and score to other players, as Play Games leaderboards do. Your Voyage progress and a few counts (ships sent, three-star finishes, dailies solved, crane lifts, your best Night Shift) go to Play's Game Stats.
  • Your name. The game reads your Play Games name only to show it in Settings, and never stores it.
  • Deleting it. The slot, achievements and scores belong to your Google account. Google's Play Games settings let you delete a game's data.

Google's handling of that data: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Purchases (Google Play Billing)

Coins, cranes, boosters, a starter pack, remove-ads, a season's premium pass and a streak freeze can be bought. The purchase runs inside Google Play's own sheet: no card number, billing address or payment detail ever reaches this game or its developer. The game learns which product was bought, so it can grant it once, and keeps the transaction id in your save. Before granting it, the game checks Google Play's signature on the purchase, on your device; that check sends nothing and collects nothing. The store sheet's Restore purchases asks Play for what you already own. Firebase Analytics, where it is allowed to run, records that a purchase was granted (the product).

No accounts, no profiles

Boxtide has no login of its own and builds no profile of you. It does not sell data, does not share it with data brokers, and has no advertising partner beyond AdMob. What AdMob does with the data it receives, including personalised ads where your consent allows it, is set out above and governed by Google's ad-data practices.

Children

Boxtide is a calm puzzle game for adults and teenagers. It is not directed at children under 13, its audience is 13 and over, and it is not in Google Play's Designed for Families programme. The game tells AdMob that its users are not under the age of consent and that its requests are not child-directed. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game and data reached one of the services above, write to the contact address and we will help you remove it.

Your choices, in one list

  • Switch off Share gameplay stats in Settings: no gameplay stats or crash reports from then on. Declining storage on your device in the consent form, where it is shown, does the same.
  • Decline personalised ads in the consent form where it is shown, and change that answer later in Settings > Ad privacy.
  • Delete or opt out of your advertising ID in Android's Settings > Privacy > Ads.
  • Buy Remove ads to stop interstitial ads.
  • Never sign in to Play Games, and nothing is sent to it.
  • Delete the game's Play Games data from your Google account's Play Games settings.
  • Clear the app's storage in Android's settings, or uninstall Boxtide: Android removes every file listed above.
